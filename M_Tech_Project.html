M.Tech Thesis Project • IIT (ISM) Dhanbad

ANALYSIS OF DUAL SPOOL VALVE CONTROL SYSTEM IN HEAVY EARTH MOVING MACHINERY

Project Guide Prof. Jayanta Das
An advanced research and performance evaluation of dual-spool hydraulic valve control architectures in heavy earth-moving machinery. This project focuses on mathematical modeling, flow characteristic decoupling, and experimental simulation to optimize precision and energy efficiency in complex hydraulic power systems.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & OBJECTIVE

Traditional single-spool hydraulic systems tightly couple metering edges, restricting independent control over flow rates and actuator pressures. In heavy earth-moving machinery (HEMM), this coupling limits operational precision and introduces unnecessary throttling losses. The objective of this research was to model and analyze a Dual Spool Valve System capable of decoupled metering, thereby enhancing dynamic response, stability, and energy utilization under heavy mechanical loads.

TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION & SIMULATION

The project was executed through rigorous mathematical modeling, circuit simulation, and experimental data correlation:

  • Dynamic System Modeling: Formulated non-linear differential equations capturing fluid flow dynamics, pressure transients, and spool displacement mechanics across valve chambers.
  • Control System Simulation: Developed comprehensive simulation models in MATLAB / Simulink to evaluate multi-input multi-output (MIMO) valve behaviors and independent spool actuation.
  • Experimental Benchmarking: Processed empirical test-bench data—including flow rate variations at rod/cap ends and pressure distribution curves—to validate model fidelity against physical phenomena.

RESULTS & PERFORMANCE IMPACT

The comparative evaluation between single and dual spool configurations highlighted substantial performance gains:

  • Achieved independent control of meter-in and meter-out orifices, successfully eliminating counter-pressure anomalies.
  • Demonstrated faster transient displacement response and superior vibration damping under sudden load variations.
  • Quantified significant reductions in pressure drops and energy waste, validating the dual-spool architecture for high-efficiency electro-hydraulic systems.
