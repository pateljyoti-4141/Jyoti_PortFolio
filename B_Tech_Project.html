B.Tech Final Year Project

DESIGN AND FABRICATION OF AN AUTOMATIC PAPER CUTTING MACHINE USING A GENEVA MECHANISM

Project Guide Dr. Akhilesh Kumar Chauhan
Design and fabrication of an automatic paper cutting machine using a Geneva mechanism to convert continuous rotary motion into precise intermittent motion. The prototype eliminates manual marking errors and provides accurate, repeatable cutting for small-scale industrial applications.

OBJECTIVES

  • Design a machine capable of cutting paper into different sizes with high accuracy.
  • Eliminate manual paper marking and measurement errors.
  • Develop a low-cost, purely mechanical solution without electronic control systems.
  • Validate operational efficiency through comprehensive testing.

MECHANISM

The machine uses a Geneva mechanism (Maltese cross) to convert continuous rotation into stepped intermittent motion. Key components include:

  • Geneva Wheel: Four-slot intermittent drive wheel advancing paper in discrete steps.
  • Crank & Lever: Converts rotational motion to linear cutting action.
  • Spring Return: Restores cutter to initial position after each stroke.
  • Gear Train: Provides speed reduction and torque optimization.

IMPLEMENTATION

  • Design Phase: CAD modeling with PTC Creo and kinematic simulations to verify motion profiles.
  • Fabrication: Precision machining and assembly of all components with optimized material selection.
  • Testing: Validation of cutting accuracy, repeatability, and safety under continuous operation.

KEY ACHIEVEMENTS

  • Achieved consistent cutting dimensions with minimal deviation across operational cycles.
  • Implemented reliable mechanical solution requiring minimal maintenance.
  • Significantly reduced paper preparation time compared to manual methods.
  • Incorporated safety mechanisms for operator protection.
